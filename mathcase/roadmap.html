Ways to do mathematics

A subject tells you what a question is about. You can work with that question in several ways.

  • Solve or check an answer. Choose three fractions and check whether they add up to 4/21.
  • Pose a new question. What happens if the divisor of a square can be any number? Try changing the prime condition.
  • Compare explanations for elegance. Read the product argument and the remainder argument. Which makes the conclusion feel inevitable to you? Compare the explanations.
  • Understand why. A checker finds one triangle. Follow one dot’s five lines to see why every coloring must contain one.
  • Reuse a result. A fact about prime divisors also does a job inside a larger proof. See how it helps prove √2 is irrational.
  • Choose a next investigation. A particular 101-dot graph cannot gain a dot with all its old lines fixed. Could a different arrangement work? That construction remains an open direction.

What remains open?

Collatz sequencesWhich starting numbers are guaranteed to drop?

One rule guarantees a drop within 37 ordinary steps for a particular collection of starting numbers. On odd inputs it uses a shortcut that joins “triple and add 1” with a single halving. A computer count combined this rule with four others. A single proof covering that combination remains open, as does the question of whether every positive start reaches 1.

Graph coloring · RamseyCould a different coloring work where this one fails?

A known 42-dot coloring avoids five dots whose lines are all the same color. Lean checked it. Independent review remains open. Another known coloring on 101 dots avoids a one-color group of six. A computer ruled out adding a dot while keeping the old lines fixed. Could a different arrangement work?

Fractions · Erdős–StrausWhich denominators admit a single formula?

The fraction search recorded 831,860 exact examples in a filtered set. Separate algebraic formulas explain whole families, including numbers of the form 28k + 21. A formula covering every integer n ≥ 2 remains the open Erdős–Straus question.

Future subjects and explanations

The longer-term aim is to make more areas of mathematics approachable at different levels. Geometry, calculus and probability are examples of areas beyond the current guided case catalog. A planned geometry example asks why the midpoint of a right triangle’s longest side is the same distance from all three corners. Its worked explanation and check are still to come.

Today you can read the prime-divisibility argument at five levels on this site. The source also describes five historical drafts for a theorem about lists. Independent teaching review and evidence about what readers learned remain open.
