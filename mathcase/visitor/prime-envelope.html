Number theory · your first bounded case

How close can a fraction get to its smallest possible largest denominator?

Write 4/p as three different unit fractions, 1/x + 1/y + 1/z. This case asks a smaller, precise question: for your prime p, can the largest denominator z fit below a stated cap?

What MathCase is checking

The question. The denominators must be positive and strictly ordered: 0 < x < y < z. We test a cap chosen from the pinned theorem, not every possible denominator.

The result. A green answer means the theorem's arithmetic criterion found the one allowed triple under this cap. A blue answer means the theorem proves there is no triple under this cap. A gray answer means the hypotheses do not justify a conclusion.

What is formal. The general floor, offset classification, and uniqueness statements were checked once in the pinned Lean source. This page does local exact integer arithmetic for your input; it does not start a new Lean proof for each input.

What the labels mean

Checked witness under the cap
We found the exact three denominators and checked the fraction identity with integers.
Proved exclusion under the cap
The pinned theorem says no allowed offset reaches this cap. A larger denominator may still work.
Inconclusive or out of scope
The input is composite, outside the theorem's range, or fails a size condition. MathCase does not guess.
