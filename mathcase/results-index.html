November 27, 2026 · How small can the largest Egyptian-fraction denominator be?

Write 4/p as a sum of three unit fractions with distinct denominators x < y < z, where p ≥ 43 is prime. For p = 881 the smallest possible z is 16,739. For the next prime, 883, even 16,777 is too small.

Let D(b) = 4b² − 6b + 1 and let B be the first integer b ≥ 2 with D(b) ≥ p. The written proof gives the lower bound z ≥ Bp. When B ≥ 16 and (s + 1)² ≤ B for a whole number s ≥ 0, it also determines whether a triple fits below or at (B + s)p. Any triple in that range is unique. A separate integer calculation checked all 78,485 primes from 43 through one million.

Monks and Velingker established the leading lower bound and characterized fixed multiplier pairs in 2008. The questions for review are the exact offsets near the lower bound, uniqueness in that range, and how rarely primes attain it. Read the proof, comparison and replay · Read the 2008 paper.

Try your own prime

Choose a prime from 43 through 1,000,000. Leave s blank for the widest range with a uniqueness guarantee. In the centered window you can enter a larger width to list every triple while the size bounds hold.

Examples: the original window finds (228, 10572, 16739) for p = 881; the wider criterion finds (420, 24555, 45836) for p = 1637 with s = 7. For p = 887 with s = 7, its theorem-side upper condition fails and the checker stops without claiming a result.

November 26, 2026 · How the order of odd steps changes a Collatz first drop

With at most 9 odd inputs in the first 12 shortcut steps and 6 in the next 12, every n ≥ 3 has a strict first drop Tk(n) < n within 24 shortcut steps, or 39 ordinary Collatz steps. The bound is attained at n = 283. With caps of 6 odd inputs and then 9, every n ≥ 3 drops within 10 shortcut steps, or 16 ordinary steps, attained at n = 347. Each filter covers 10,082,670 parity classes. The receipt also checks that a first-window-only cap of 6 gives the same 10/16 bound. These are class-wide bounded results, not a proof of global convergence. November 26, 2026 · Testing 17,500 five-color patterns for Weak Schur 208

An exhaustive screen checked 17,500 translations in total across 30 primes q ≡ 1 (mod 5), from 211 through 1,000. Every translation has a monochromatic triple with distinct addends using only nonzero residues, so assigning a color to a zero position cannot repair it. A separate checker recomputed the prime and root choices and all constraints. This excludes only the stated multiplicative-coset family from supplying a five-coloring of 1–208. It does not rule out arbitrary colorings or show that 208 is impossible. The established lower bound remains 207. November 26, 2026 · What would a five-coloring of 208 have to change?

Relative to each of two known five-color partitions of 1–207, any valid coloring of 1–208 would have to change membership in at least four of the five old color classes, even after color names are permuted. Twenty separate three-free-color cases have checked DRAT refutations, verified on demand against reconstructed formulas. A separate four-free-color search gave no verdict. This restricts colorings built from those two seeds; it does not show that 208 cannot be colored or change the known lower bound 207. November 26, 2026 · The Egyptian-fraction bound scales to multiples

Scaling the 143-prime result shows that the 1,032n bound on the smallest possible largest denominator holds for all multiples of primes p ≤ 10,000 with p ≡ 1 (mod 24). This union of multiples has natural density 8.52026928%. The prime n = 9,601 still attains the bound, so it is sharp on this union. This corollary applies to the specified multiples, not all integers. Read the scaling argument and scope.

November 24, 2026 · A fixed-endpoint Collatz descent theorem

The Lean statement proves T23(n) < n for n ≥ 3 when at most 14 of the first 23 shortcut steps start odd. A shortcut step sends even n to n/2 and odd n to (3n+1)/2. In ordinary steps, the drop occurs after 23 + h steps, where h is the number of odd starts in those shortcuts. Earlier work already implies this result: Terras (1976, p. 252 and Theorem 1.2) gives a drop within 37 ordinary steps, and Rozier and Terracol (Theorem 5.1 and Table 1) imply the same fixed endpoint. MathCase records a formalization of a known consequence, not a first proof or a solution to the conjecture. Read the statement, example, and proof links; see the dated research timeline.

Erdős–Straus fractions

Across 100 ranges through d = 10,000,000, an exact witness was recorded for all 831,860 factor-filtered candidate rows; 427 first-pass misses were repaired within the 65,536 offset cap. A separate minimum-denominator study exhaustively checked all 143 primes p ≤ 10,000 with p ≡ 1 (mod 24), finding z ≤ 1,032p and a unique extremal p = 9,601. These finite results do not settle the conjecture for every integer. Read the campaign and minimum-denominator results · See the separate 433 + 840t identity.

Finite surface homology over F₂

An exact computation for three explicit triangulations gives first Betti numbers 0 for the sphere, 2 for the torus, and 1 for the real projective plane. Boundary maps, their ranks, and structural checks were recomputed over F₂. This is a finite example, not a general topology result. Read the method and limits · inspect the saved verification.

Integer Smith-normal-form method audit

Two exact computations agreed on 130 deterministic integer matrices, and a deliberately changed answer was caught. This is a bounded method audit, distinct from the finite F₂ surface calculation and not a proof for every integer matrix. Read the corrected audit and replay receipt.

Periodic square-lattice Ising counts

For the zero-field antiferromagnetic model on a periodic 5×5 square lattice, an exact enumeration checked all 33,554,432 spin assignments and found 2,470 ground-state arrangements. A row-transfer calculation and Gray-code enumeration agreed across every energy bin. For the 3×10 periodic lattice, two methods counted every one of 1,073,741,824 spin arrangements by energy and magnetization, and all six lowest-energy arrangements have 15 spins up and 15 spins down. This is a finite computation and reference implementation, not a physics-priority claim. Read the model and scope · inspect the earlier energy-only receipt · read the joint energy-and-magnetization replay.

Local intersections of binomial plane curves

For example, over F₅ the branches x = y² and x² = y³ have isolated local intersection multiplicity 3, while x = y² and x² = y⁵ have multiplicity 4. Exact Macaulay quotient dimensions match the classical resultant formula across eleven listed cases over F₂, F₃, and F₅. An independent graph sweep checks 1,596 isolated tuples and separately records 105 shared-component tuples. This is a finite implementation check of a classical formula.
