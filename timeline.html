What happened, and what it tells us

The labels distinguish a checked theorem from a finite computation or an engineering replay. A result is only as broad as its exact statement and evidence.

  1. November 24, 2026

    Collatz · Lean-checked parameter theorem

    For most 23-bit parity patterns in this exact rule, the sequence drops below its start by step 37.

    The theorem covers starts n ≥ 3 whose first 23 accelerated inputs contain at most 14 odd values. Lean counts exactly 7,507,638 of 8,388,608 residue classes under that condition. Three nearby parameter checks remain exploratory.

    Established: the exact bounded statement, its residue count, and the recorded Lean receipt. Not established: eventual descent for every integer, a smaller combined proof basis, or the Collatz conjecture.

  2. November 24, 2026

    M31 · arithmetic provenance repair

    The exact original protocol bytes now match the step-34 run receipts.

    The step-34 finite calculation and independent recomputation were already recorded as passing. The package had failed text hygiene because its Markdown protocol inputs used CRLF. A byte-binding audit now connects the preserved original bytes to the hashes recorded by both runs; it did not rerun the calculation.

    Established: exact input-byte provenance for the existing receipts and preservation of the packaging failure. Not established: class-wide descent, later behavior, or Collatz convergence.

  3. October 2026

    Calibration · saved proof case

    A known prime-divisibility statement exposed both a bad attempt and a repair.

    MathCase preserved a candidate that Lean rejected, then the corrected proof that Lean accepted for the exact prime statement. A separate composite example shows why the primality condition matters.

    Established: the saved checker path can retain and display a failed attempt beside a passing proof for a known result. Not established: a new theorem or broad open-problem solving ability.

    Collatz · finite residue evidence

    Bounded first-descent records grew across several residue moduli.

    The public record includes a Lean source covering 7,825 of 8,192 residue classes within 21 steps. A larger chain reports 1,021,248 of 1,048,576 classes with a listed bounded descent.

    Established: finite coverage counts and selected bounded proofs, with their separate evidence types. Not established: that every integer descends or that the Collatz conjecture is solved.

  5. November 12–13, 2026

    Pinned replay · composition evidence

    The shard set was replayed, and the largest residue tier was recorded in chunks.

    The preserved record covers 229 shard modules. The 2²⁰ result combines 133 intermediate modules with the existing final composition theorem; earlier all-at-once and elaboration attempts remain in the history.

    Established: recorded source, receipt, and bounded theorem evidence. Not established: a global Collatz result or an anonymous clean-room build.

  6. November 18, 2026

    Fresh source replay · bounded theorem

    Two offline runs agreed on the 363-file source replay.

    Each run compiled the 229 shard inputs and 134 composition files, checked 23 axiom groups across 4,262 queries, and reported zero sorryAx. All per-module source, output, and exit-code hashes agree between the runs. The first Docker wrapper ended with 124 after its watchdog expired; the second returned 0.

    Established: agreement between the two recorded runs in the pinned environment. Not established: that a stranger can repeat the build; the image is not anonymously pullable and the complete rebuild context is missing.

  7. November 18, 2026

    Erdős–Straus · exact finite computation

    The public campaign now includes 831,860 witnesses through d = 10,000,000.

    The fixed factor-conditioned candidate set has one exact witness per recorded candidate. All 427 bounded base-search misses were resolved within the recorded cap. The miss analysis found no single pattern covering the cases and no new identity family.

    Established: checked finite identities for this filtered set and range. Not established: a decomposition for every integer or a proof of the Erdős–Straus conjecture.

    Finite graph · reproduction of known work

    A 42-vertex construction was checked against every five-vertex subset.

    The browser checker checks the known 42-vertex construction across 850,668 five-vertex subsets. A separate Lean formalization then checked the exact lower-bound target in 27 chunks and a final composition.

    Established: a Lean-checked formalization of the known R(5,5) ≥ 43 witness, using a warm local cache. Not established: a new construction, the exact Ramsey number, a clean-checkout replay, or dossier acceptance.
