Real questions · bounded evidence · next steps

Six math questions, and exactly how far the checks go.

MathCase has runnable work on number patterns, fractions, and graph colorings. Each card starts with the plain question, then tells you what was checked, what remains open, and the next concrete target.

Van der Waerden · number patterns

Can you avoid seven equally spaced numbers of one color?

These numbers can skip positions: for example, 2, 5, and 8 are equally spaced. A checked two-coloring covers 3,703 positions without a single-color pattern of seven. That reproduces the known lower bound W(2,7) ≥ 3,704.

MathCase also checked 617 cases in one specified quadratic-residue construction family at length 3,704, covering 644,907,008 assignments. That family is ruled out; other colorings are not.

Next: check a different construction family, or produce a general certificate covering every coloring at length 3,704.

Weak Schur · five colors

Can five colors cover 1 through 208 without a same-color sum?

The rule forbids same-color sums a + b = c when a and b are distinct positive numbers. Two known colorings cover 1 through 207; MathCase checked both and searched every color for 208 while changing up to 18 old entries.

All ten bounded searches finished. Starting from either published coloring, at least 19 old positions must change before an extension can work. A coloring farther from both starting points could still exist.

Next: find a valid 208 coloring outside those neighborhoods, or check a proof that covers every five-coloring.

Run the witness and radius checks ↗ Rowley's published sequence record ↗ Vicenzi et al., 2026 ↗

Ramsey · dots and lines

Can you color lines between 51 dots without making a one-color triangle?

A known graph on 50 vertices has no monochromatic triangle, giving R(3,3,3,3) ≥ 51. MathCase checked every triangle and every way to attach a 51st vertex while leaving the old graph unchanged.

A second bounded check found that an extension from this particular starting graph needs at least three old edges recolored. The published interval is 51–60; MathCase did not reproduce the upper-bound proof.

Next: search for a genuinely different 50-vertex starting graph or expand the certified class of possible starts.

Run the graph and certificate checks ↗ Chung's construction ↗ Tatarević's version 2 paper ↗

Van der Waerden · number patterns

Can five colors avoid three equally spaced positions of one color through 171?

A checked coloring covers 170 positions without a monochromatic three-term progression, so W(5,3) ≥ 171. The unrestricted 171-position search has 855 variables and 38,006 clauses.

The 20-second solver probe ended unknown; it did not prove that 171 is impossible. A small W(2,3)=9 positive control was independently checked.

Next: obtain a checked result for the unrestricted 171-position formula, or find an avoiding coloring there.

Run the witness and positive-control checks ↗ Heule's van der Waerden paper ↗

Fractions · three unit fractions

Can 4/21 be written as three fractions with 1 on top?

Yes. Two exact examples are 4/21 = 1/7 + 1/22 + 1/462 and 4/21 = 1/6 + 1/63 + 1/126. The checked formulas extend to every number of the form 28k+21.

A separate formula covers every positive multiple of seven. These are exact identities for specific infinite families, not a solution of the Erdős–Straus conjecture for every integer.

Next: finish the literature comparison before making any broader identity or priority claim.

Ramsey · dots and lines

Can you add a 102nd dot without making six dots whose lines are all one color?

The known Paley graph on 101 vertices has no monochromatic six-clique, giving R(6,6) ≥ 102. A checked certificate rules out adding one new vertex while keeping every old edge the same.

Other bounded checks find that at least 16 old edges must change. None of 33 predefined structured edge-change patterns gave a valid extension; each produced a six-clique. These restrictions apply to this starting graph and these changes only.

Next: find a materially different 102-vertex coloring and check every six-vertex subset.

For people who want the evidence

Every card has a runnable check and a written limit.

Clone MathCase, then run `python scripts/run_frontier_case.py list`. Choose an ID to produce a fresh run folder with logs and hashes. The checks use local Python and, for the fraction identities, Lean 4.33.1. The weak-Schur check downloads only its two hash-pinned public witnesses. No model calls are part of these runs.

Open the complete research package ↗

No claim here says Collatz, a Ramsey problem, van der Waerden, weak Schur, or Erdős–Straus is solved. The listed bounds and identities have the narrower scopes stated on their cards.
