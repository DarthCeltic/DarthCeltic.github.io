How to read a result

A check applies to one exact statement.

For the prime-divisibility example, the saved record includes an actual Lean rejection and a repaired candidate. That makes the proof process inspectable without turning a known theorem into a discovery claim.

In plain language

If a natural prime p divides n squared, then p divides n.

p prime, p ∣ n² ⟹ p ∣ n

What it proves: the exact formal theorem was accepted by the saved Lean check.
What it does not prove: novelty, independent review of every explanation, or open-problem capability.

Latest research update · Nov 20, 2026

The bounded Collatz replay sits beside two finite Ramsey checks.

MathCase publishes a Lean proof of the known lower bound R(5,5) ≥ 43 from a 42-vertex graph, plus a separate two-checker finite certificate for the known lower bound R(6,6) ≥ 102 from a 101-vertex graph. The R(6,6) check is computational evidence, not Lean-checked and not a new construction. The Collatz result remains exact and bounded: 2,652 listed progressions descend after 32 ordinary steps, with two agreeing source replays. The R(5,5) Lean package still awaits a fresh-checkout rebuild.
