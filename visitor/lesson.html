Start here: 4 divides 2 squared, but 4 does not divide 2. That one example shows why the unrestricted rule fails.

First, try 4 and 2

What happens: 4 divides 2 × 2, but 4 does not divide 2.

Exact Lean statement(4 : Nat) ∣ 2 ^ 2 ∧ ¬ (4 : Nat) ∣ 2

Shows: the unrestricted implication fails: 4 divides 2², but 4 does not divide 2.

Next, assume the divisor is prime

Plain: If a prime divides a natural number’s square, it divides that number.

Exact Lean statement∀ (p n : Nat), p.Prime → p ∣ n ^ 2 → p ∣ n

Shows: a known elementary theorem, checked by Lean from the Mathlib prime-divisor lemma.

Does not show: that every composite divisor fails, a new result, or a general AI theorem-solving capability.
