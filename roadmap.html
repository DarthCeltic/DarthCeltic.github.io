Work that can survive outside this machine

MathCase is building a tool mathematicians can inspect, reproduce, and use.

The strongest evidence today is a disciplined checking and record-keeping pipeline. Broad autonomous mathematical discovery has not been demonstrated. This roadmap turns that gap into specific work with visible finish conditions.

  3. 03

    Lean-checked known bound · independent review open

    Choose one finite-structure target only after checking its statement and prior work.

    The known R(5,5) lower bound now has a MathCase Lean formalization: 27 chunks and a final composition checked the 42-vertex witness. Ryan reviewed the exact statement (M0 YES). The run used a warm local cache; fresh-checkout reproduction and independent review remain open. R(6,6) is staged with M0 YES, but no construction search or Lean attempt is authorized yet.

    R(5,5) Lean target: ∃ G : SimpleGraph (Fin 42), G.CliqueFree 5 ∧ (Gᶜ).CliqueFree 5.

    Why not call this a new result?

    The graph construction and bound are known. The browser checker verifies the finite certificate, and MathCase has a separate Lean proof bundle. The upstream FormalConjectures file remains a proof placeholder; that does not change the saved MathCase result or make it a new construction.

  4. 04

    Computation complete · proof idea not established

    Use the Erdős–Straus pile to look for a proved identity family.

    The public data now includes 831,860 exact witnesses for a filtered finite set through d = 10,000,000. Its miss analysis did not establish a new general rule. The next useful step is to formulate one precise candidate identity or criterion from the existing pile, then prove it and check prior work.

    What would count as progress?

    A new identity pattern is only a conjecture until its exact hypotheses and algebraic proof are written down. A literature check must precede any novelty claim. More range extension alone is not the headline.

    Read the complete campaign scope and limits →
  5. 05

    Staged · no model calls running

    Test whether a checked library helps on held-out theorems.

    A useful comparison needs the same Lean snapshot, fixed held-out targets, a library-enabled arm, a no-library baseline, and equal time and attempt budgets. The 11 reserved evaluation bundles remain unopened so they can still serve as held-out material.

    Why is there no score yet?

    The comparison depends on a model-based worker. No provider or model calls are running, no target has been attempted for this measurement, and external spend is $0. Until the necessary review and execution conditions exist, a transfer score would be invented.
