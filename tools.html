Fractions

Can 4/21 be three fractions with 1 on top?

Try the worked example. The checker multiplies through by the denominators, so it uses exact whole-number arithmetic.

Example: 4/21 = 1/7 + 1/22 + 1/462.

Why the exact check works

4xyz = 21(xy + xz + yz)

For positive increasing denominators x, y, z, this integer equality is exactly equivalent to 4/21 = 1/x + 1/y + 1/z.

What this shows: whether these three chosen fractions add exactly to 4/21. It does not prove a formula for every denominator or solve the full Erdős–Straus conjecture.

More exploration

Search within chosen fraction bounds

For n = 12d+1, MathCase searches your finite denominator bounds for positive, strictly increasing x, y, z that satisfy the exact equation.

4/n = 1/x + 1/y + 1/z

Ready. The search is bounded and stays in this browser.

What a witness means: the displayed tuple satisfies the exact integer identity for this one n. A bounded miss means only that no witness appeared within the searched bounds; it is not a counterexample to the conjecture.

About the receipts

Each receipt records its inputs, finite bound, exact result, and a SHA-256 digest over the canonical result fields. Downloading saves a JSON file to your device. Saving stores the receipt in this browser's local storage only; it is not uploaded or shared.

This tools page does not compile Lean or present arithmetic as a kernel-checked proof. The separate visitor workbench edits Lean source and supports a compiler-only receipt; that receipt can still rely on declared axioms and does not contain an axiom audit. Use the pinned compiler in your own Codespace when you want the reproducible local route.
